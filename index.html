    What Your Child Actually Needs

    Communication develops step by step. It starts long before words, with eye contact, shared attention, and pointing. Then comes labeling, understanding of concepts like opposites, and answering questions. Word Tube follows this natural path.

    First: Labeling

    Once your child recognizes words, they can start labeling. Animals, food, colors, family members. Simple, familiar things from their everyday world.

    Then: Understanding

    Before a child can say a word, they need to understand it. Word Tube starts with receptive exercises: matching pictures to sounds, learning what words mean.

    Next: Understanding Concepts

    Big and small. One and many. Hot and cold. Word Tube builds understanding of concepts through visual comparisons your child can practice again and again.

    Then: Answering Questions

    "What is this?" "What color?" "Where is the dog?" Answering questions is a major communication milestone. Word Tube practices it in a calm, structured way.

    Repetition Makes It Stick

    Children with speech delay need to see and hear a word hundreds of times before it clicks. Word Tube is built around repetition. Your child swipes through exercises at their own pace.

    Your Voice, Your Photos

    Record words in your own voice. Add photos of your child's toys, food, and family. Familiar content from their world helps words stick faster.

    Why This App Exists

    Word Tube wasn't built in an office. It was built at a kitchen table, between therapy appointments, by a parent who needed something that didn't exist.

    1

    Ana wasn't speaking

    At two years old, our daughter had one word. No pointing, no eye contact, no consistent communication. We didn't know where to turn.

    2

    We started with exercises at home

    Flashcards, picture books, naming games. Guided by Temple Grandin's thinking and what we learned from occupational therapy sessions. Repetition was the key to everything.

    3

    I turned those exercises into an app

    As a software developer, I built Word Tube so Ana could practice in a calm, familiar environment. Her older sister Nika drew many of the illustrations.
