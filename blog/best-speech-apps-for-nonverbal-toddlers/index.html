    Best Speech Apps for Nonverbal Toddlers: How to Choose

    September 8, 2026 · Word Tube

    There isn't one best speech app for a nonverbal toddler. There's the app that fits where your child is right now. Most apps for children with few or no words fall into three types. Speech practice apps invite a child to copy sounds and words. AAC apps (augmentative and alternative communication) give a child a way to say things today by tapping pictures that speak out loud. First-words and picture apps build understanding by showing a picture and saying its word, again and again. Some families use more than one.

    Below is a simple way to choose, a side-by-side table, and what each app is built for. We checked how each one is paid for on its official store page or website in October 2026. That can change, so look again before you pay.

    A note on words: some families prefer "nonspeaking" to "nonverbal". We use "nonverbal" here because it's the word many parents search for.

    How to Choose: Four Questions

    1. What can your child do today?

    • Not pointing yet, understands only a few words. Start with understanding. A picture with its word, lots of repetition, and you sitting right there. Some families find that apps asking a child to repeat words are a few steps ahead for now. AAC can start here too. There are no prerequisites for it.
    • Points and understands a fair amount, but has few or no words. Picture and word practice still helps, and this is a good moment to ask about AAC, if you haven't already.
    • Copies sounds or says some words. Speech practice apps with video models or voice games make more sense here.

    Not sure? Our guide for a toddler who isn't talking or pointing yet walks through how to spot the step your child is on.

    2. Does your child need a way to say things now?

    If your child has a lot to say and no way to say it, AAC is worth raising with a speech-language pathologist now, not later. AAC isn't a last resort and it isn't giving up on speech. The American Speech-Language-Hearing Association says "there are no prerequisites for AAC intervention" and that "early AAC use can help develop speech and language" (ASHA). Your child doesn't have to "earn" it by trying other things first.

    3. What's your budget?

    Practice apps are usually free to download with a subscription. Full AAC apps are often a large one-time purchase, sometimes with paid add-ons. Costs vary a lot, and there are free options of both kinds.

    4. Does your child engage with screens?

    Some children light up with a tablet. Some hand it back after ten seconds. If yours ignores apps, there's usually a reason you can name, and we cover the common ones in my child ignores speech apps. Whatever you pick, it works better with you sitting next to them.

    Two small checks before you subscribe. Scroll to "App Privacy" on the store page, because the labels differ a lot between apps. And if there's a free trial, put a reminder in your phone the day before it ends.

    The Apps, One by One

    Word Tube

    Word Tube was built by a dad for his daughter, using the picture and word exercises the family practiced at home. A child sees a photo, hears the word and answers with a tap or a swipe, in a calm layout that feels like the video apps many toddlers already know.

    Good fit: a child who isn't talking yet and needs lots of repetition to build understanding, alongside therapy or while you wait for it.

    Word Tube: free download, monthly or yearly subscription with a free trial.

    Speech Blubs

    Its core is video modeling: real children on video say sounds and words, and your child is invited to copy them. It also has voice-controlled activities and face filters that use the camera. The store listing says 1,500+ exercises and describes it as for kids and toddlers aged 1 to 8 (App Store).

    Good fit: a child who already copies sounds or actions and enjoys watching other children.

    Free to download, with a monthly or yearly subscription that starts with a free trial (Speech Blubs).

    Sago Mini First Words

    From Piknik. Its store subtitle is "Articulation and speech games", and it's listed for ages 0 to 5 (App Store).

    Good fit: a late talker who already enjoys playful kids' games and is starting to copy words.

    Free to download, with a weekly, monthly or yearly subscription and a free trial.

    Speech Therapy 1 Preverbal

    From ImagiRation. The child uses their voice to interact with animals, lights and stars on the screen. It's aimed at preverbal and nonverbal children. It was free with no in-app purchases listed when we checked, and the listing says it's designed for iPad (App Store).

    Good fit: a child who makes sounds and loves cause and effect. It's a nice way to show them their voice does something.

    Proloquo2Go

    A full AAC app from AssistiveWare. Your child taps symbols or types, and the device speaks. It's built for people "who cannot speak or need help being understood" (App Store).

    Good fit: a child who needs a full system to tell you things now, ideally set up with an SLP. Families on Apple devices.

    A one-time purchase, with optional in-app purchases. It's Apple only (AssistiveWare).

    TouchChat HD and LAMP Words for Life

    Both are full AAC apps from the Prentke Romich Company. TouchChat HD is a one-time purchase, with vocabulary sets sold separately as in-app purchases (App Store). LAMP Words for Life is also a one-time purchase (App Store) and is built around consistent motor planning. LAMP has a free trial version called Discover (LAMP Words for Life).

    Good fit: families choosing an AAC system together with their SLP, or matching what their child already uses at school or therapy.

    Cboard

    A free, open source AAC board app. It runs in a web browser and has apps for iPhone, iPad, Android and Windows. You can build boards with your own pictures (Cboard). An optional Pro subscription, monthly or yearly, adds things like recording your own voice for tiles, more natural voices, sharing boards, and no ads (Cboard pricing).

    Good fit: trying AAC without spending money, or a backup board on a second device.

    Getting Started

    Pick one app that matches your child's stage today. Not three. Use it for ten minutes a day, sitting next to your child, for about three weeks before you judge it. Keep a note on your phone of any new sound, point or word, because slow progress is hard to see day to day.

    And keep the off-screen practice going. Pointing games, picture books and everyday moments matter too. You'll find simple ideas in speech therapy exercises at home and what to do while you wait for speech therapy.

    None of these apps replaces a speech-language pathologist. They're for practice in between.

    Common Questions

    What is the best speech app for a nonverbal toddler?

    It depends on what your child can do today. A child who isn't pointing yet may get the most from picture and word practice that builds understanding, and AAC is an option at any stage. A child who copies sounds may enjoy speech practice apps with video models. A child who needs to communicate now may benefit from an AAC app, ideally chosen with a speech-language pathologist.

    Are AAC apps or speech apps better for nonverbal children?

    They do different jobs. Speech practice apps invite a child to copy sounds and words. AAC apps give a child a way to communicate right now by tapping pictures that speak. You don't have to choose one, and the American Speech-Language-Hearing Association says there are no prerequisites for AAC.

    Will an AAC app stop my child from learning to talk?

    According to ASHA, early AAC use can help develop speech and language. A guide from the Communication Health Support Association, powered by ASHA, says "AAC helps develop language and enhance speech" and that people who use AAC can use both speech and AAC. It's a support for communication, not a last resort.

    Is there a free speech app for nonverbal toddlers?

    Yes. Cboard is a free, open source AAC board app with an optional paid Pro plan. Speech Therapy 1 Preverbal was free on iPad when checked in October 2026. Most practice apps, including Word Tube and Speech Blubs, are free to download with a paid subscription and a free trial.

    Can a speech app replace speech therapy?

    No. An app is a tool for practice at home, alongside therapy or while you wait for it. A speech-language pathologist can assess your child, suggest goals and help you choose and set up the right tools, including AAC.

    Which app is best for a toddler who understands but doesn't talk?

    Look for an app that builds on what your child already understands, with lots of repetition of pictures and words, and pair it with simple ways to ask, like pointing or signs. If your child has a lot to say and no way to say it, ask a speech-language pathologist about AAC too.
